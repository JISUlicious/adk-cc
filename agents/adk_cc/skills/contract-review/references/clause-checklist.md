# Clause checklist — what to look for and why

Concepts here are general practice. Whether any of it is *enforceable*, or
overridden by mandatory local rules, is jurisdiction-specific — verify or refer.

## Money and performance
- **Scope / deliverables** — is acceptance objective, or "to the satisfaction
  of"? Subjective acceptance is a payment risk.
- **Payment** — trigger, timing, disputes, late consequences, expenses, taxes
  (who bears withholding — a common cross-border surprise).
- **Change control** — how scope changes get priced, or whether they do.

## Risk allocation *(usually the highest-value clauses)*
- **Limitation of liability** — is there a cap, is it mutual, what is carved
  out of it? Uncapped exposure is the classic blocking finding.
- **Indemnities** — who defends whom, for what, who controls the defence.
  Indemnifying a counterparty for their own conduct is a red flag.
- **Insurance** — required types and amounts; can the user actually meet them?
- **Force majeure** — what counts, and what happens to payment during it.

## IP and data
- **Ownership** — of deliverables, of pre-existing material, of improvements.
  Watch for assignment where a licence was intended.
- **Licence grants** — scope, exclusivity, sublicensing, term, territory.
- **Feedback / usage rights** — often a quiet grant of broad rights.
- **Personal data** — roles, purposes, transfers, security, breach notice,
  sub-processors, deletion. Sector and jurisdiction rules bite hardest here.

## Duration and exit
- **Term, renewal, notice** — auto-renewal with a long notice window is a
  frequent trap.
- **Termination** — for cause, for convenience, cure periods, effect on fees.
- **Survival** — which obligations outlive the contract (confidentiality, IP,
  liability) — and for how long.
- **Transition assistance** — can the user actually leave?

## Relationship and control
- **Assignment / change of control** — can the counterparty transfer this to a
  competitor?
- **Exclusivity, non-compete, non-solicit** — scope in activity, geography, and
  time. Enforceability varies enormously by jurisdiction — flag, don't rule.
- **Subcontracting** — permitted, and who stays responsible?

## Process
- **Governing law and forum** — read this FIRST; it frames everything else.
- **Dispute resolution** — negotiation, mediation, arbitration (seat, rules,
  language), or courts. Costs and enforceability differ sharply.
- **Notices** — how, where, and when deemed received.
- **Entire agreement / order of precedence** — which document wins when an
  order form and an MSA disagree.

## Reading for absence
A contract of this type would normally address: liability cap, IP ownership,
data protection, termination for convenience, survival, dispute forum. If one is
missing, that silence is itself a finding — say what the default consequence
would likely be *in general terms*, and refer the specifics.
